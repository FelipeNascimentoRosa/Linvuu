Wo? Wohin? Woher? Adverbs provide information about how, where, when, to what degree, or how often an action takes place. They modify verbs or adjectives or other adverbs. An adverb expresses things like time, frequency, manner, place, quantity, or intensity. Adverbs are placed close to the word they modify (the verb, the adjective or the other adverb). Many adjectives can also function as adverbs in German. With few exceptions, adverbs don't change their appearance. The form you see in the list below is the form you use in a sentence. Adverbs of direction: Wohin? (where to?) Woher? (where from?) The difference between wohin and woher is the relative direction of movement from the standpoint of the speaker. Wohin suggests movement by the speaker in a direction away from himself/herself; woher , in contrast, suggests movement by somebody else towards / in the direction of the speaker. Word order with adverbs As a general rule, German adverbs appear in this sequence: time - manner - place , although there are some infrequent exceptions to this rule (especially in colloquial speech).
